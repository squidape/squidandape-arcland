      How the numbers work

      Where is my data stored?
      In this browser only, under localStorage. There is no account, no server and no database, and your holdings are never transmitted. The page does make two kinds of request, and neither carries them: a price lookup, and reads of the public Arc registry to see which tiles are claimed. Claiming a tile puts a tile number, your label and your wallet address on chain — never how much Bitcoin you own. It is not synced: use Backup to move your ledger to another device.
      "Your share of the world's Bitcoin"
      21,000,000 BTC divided by ~8.2 billion people is 0.00256 BTC each. The multiple is how many of those shares you hold.
      "People who could ever match you"
      21,000,000 divided by your stack. If everyone tried to own what you own, this is where Bitcoin runs out — and everyone after that is too late, at any price.
      How is the globe divided?
      Its surface is a 5,000 × 4,200 grid — exactly 21,000,000 squares, one Bitcoin each. Every one of those splits into 10 × 10 = 100 parcels of 0.01 BTC, giving 2,100,000,000 parcels in total. Zoom in far enough and the squares you see are individual hundredths of a coin. Like any world map it is an equirectangular projection, so squares near the poles are drawn narrower than squares on the equator; your land sits on the equator, where there is no distortion at all.
      How is mined supply calculated?
      From Bitcoin's own issuance schedule — 210,000 blocks per halving, starting at 50 BTC — extrapolated from a verified block height at 10 minutes per block. Nothing is fetched, so nothing can break.
      Is this financial advice?
      No. It is a scale toy. It does not know or care what Bitcoin costs.

      Where these numbers come from

      Why not read supply from the token contract?
      Because on Arc Testnet that number is meaningless. The faucet mints on demand, so the contract currently reports over 212 billion USDC and 2 trillion EURC. Those are real reads of a real contract and complete fiction as supply. Supply here comes from api.circle.com/v1/stablecoins, Circle's own published figures. The chain is used only for what it is genuinely authoritative about: your balance, and actual mint and burn activity.
      Is any of this stored?
      No. There is no account, no server and no database. Your balance is read from the chain when you connect and shown only to you — it is never written to storage and never transmitted anywhere.
      Does this site need an API key?
      No, and it could not safely have one. This is a static page with no backend, so any key placed in its JavaScript would be published to everyone who loads it. Every source used here is public and keyless.
      How fresh is it?
      Supply refreshes every minute, and each figure carries its own age. If a source fails, the last good number stays on screen with an honest staleness note rather than blanking or resetting to zero.
